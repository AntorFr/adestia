# Question bank

A menu, one block per point of the skeleton. Ask ONE question at a time, in
the person's language, always with proposals and a recommendation drawn from
what they already said. Skip any question an earlier answer has settled.

The proposals below are examples of the SHAPE a proposal takes — concrete,
contrasted, each implying a different agent. Make your own from the
conversation; never read these out as a list.

## 1. Role

- "What will you mostly ask it for — the household (notes, todo, projects),
  code, a single domain (the kitchen, the workshop)?"
- "Is there one thing it must NOT become?" — propose the neighbour it is
  likely to drift into: "a butler that starts writing code", "a code agent
  that starts managing your calendar".
- "Is it yours alone, or does someone else talk to it too?" — an instance
  shared by a household needs an address that fits everyone.

## 2. Name and figure

- "Do you already have a name in mind?" — if not, offer three, each with the
  character it carries: *Alfred* (the English butler), *Nestor* (the
  faithful house servant), a name of their own world. Never pick one.
- "Is there a figure, real or fictional, whose way of speaking you'd like it
  to borrow?" — a figure gives a whole register in one word. Note it, and
  note that only the voice is borrowed.

## 3. Address

- "*Tu* or *vous*?" — and between the two, which way: the agent may say
  *vous* while the person says *tu* (Alfred).
- "How should it call you — by your first name, a title (Monsieur, Madame),
  a nickname, nothing at all?" — and how often: "Monsieur, as a hint, not in
  every sentence".
- "Does it speak of itself as *je*, or in the third person?" — the third
  person is a strong marker (Skippy, Dobby); propose it only if the figure
  calls for it.

## 4. Register — the four axes

Ask as pairs, one or two per question, with a sample line for each pole:

- **Formal ↔ casual** — « Je m'en occupe immédiatement. » / « Je gère. »
- **Serious ↔ funny** — a plain answer, or a dry remark slipped in.
- **Respectful ↔ irreverent** — does it ever tease you? About what, never
  about what?
- **Matter-of-fact ↔ enthusiastic** — « C'est fait. » / « Et voilà, c'est
  fait ! » — exclamation marks or none.

## 5. Traits

- "Give me two or three words for how it should come across." Then, for each
  word, propose the phrase that embodies it and ask whether it sounds right.
  The phrase is what gets written, not the word.
- "Is there a way of talking that would annoy you?" — the negative traits
  are often clearer than the positive ones: flattery, enthusiasm, filler
  ("Excellente question !"), excessive caution.

## 6. Signature

- "Should it have a formula of its own — an interjection, a way of closing?"
  Offer two drawn from the figure. Then ask the dose: "every answer, now and
  then, or only when the moment calls for it?"

## 7. Initiative

- "When you ask it something, should it mention what it noticed in passing —
  a forgotten task, a stalled project?" (anticipation inside the answer)
- "Should it ever speak first, without being asked?" — default to strictly
  reactive; a scheduled note is the instance's way to speak first, not the
  persona's.

## 8. Guardrails

Do not ask for them from scratch: present the default set from `SKILL.md` in
two sentences and ask what to add or soften. Two follow-ups worth asking:

- "When something is serious — a mistake with consequences, a security
  question — should it drop the character entirely, or just tone it down?"
- "When it recommends something, do you want the why and how sure it is?"

## 9. Language and length

- "French by default? Another language for some subjects?"
- "Short answers you can ask to expand, or complete answers you can skim?"

## The samples — after 3 and 4, then again at the end

Write three short replies in the voice and ask what rings false:

1. **Ordinary** — "Ajoute du pain à ma liste de courses."
2. **Bad news** — "La sauvegarde d'hier n'a pas tourné."
3. **Disagreement** — the person proposes something the agent thinks is a
   mistake.

The bad-news sample tests "drop a notch"; the disagreement tests whether the
character can still say no. A persona that passes all three is ready.
